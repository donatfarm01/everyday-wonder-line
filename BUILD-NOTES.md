# Build notes — Everyday Wonder, Two Days to Find the Line

The entry is a single sentence about a small moment of wonder in everyday life. The program writes that one sentence to the output entry file and nothing else.

The sentence turns on a shadow arriving at a crossing a step ahead of its owner. The moment is real and specific: at low sun a shadow genuinely stretches ahead of you, so it reaches the crossing first. The surprise is the turn at the end, where the shadow waits like it knows the way home, which makes an ordinary walk feel briefly strange without leaving the everyday behind.

The first draft said the shadow reached the crossing and waited as if it already knew the way home. The revision tightened it in two places. Arrived replaced reached because it carries the sense of a journey ending. A full step was added because it pins the moment to a concrete measure, one step, instead of a vague before. For me replaced there in the waiting clause so the sentence stays addressed to the walker, not to an empty place.

The finished sentence is one hundred and four characters long, inside the one to two hundred character gate, and it is a single sentence with no trailing newline in the entry file. It avoids the examples in the bounty brief, the lift door, the kettle and the puddle, so the angle is its own.

The program itself is deliberately small. It holds the sentence as a constant, creates the output folder if it is missing, and writes the sentence as UTF-8 text. Running it takes a fraction of a second and needs no network, no input and no configuration.
